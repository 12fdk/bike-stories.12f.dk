What is Bike Stories?

Bike Stories is an iPhone app that keeps a maintenance log for every bike you own: what has been serviced, what is due next, which parts are wearing out, and what each bike has cost you per kilometre. It is free to download and needs no account.

It logs rides by hand, from Apple Health or from a GPX file, stores receipts, manuals and warranties, charts distance and spending over time, and keeps all of it on your iPhone and in your own iCloud — never on a server of ours.

How do I keep a bike maintenance log?

Add the bike, and Bike Stories starts a service history for it. Log each service with its date, cost and what was done; schedule the next one by date or by distance. The history stays with the bike — searchable years later, and worth money when you sell it.

How much does my bike cost per kilometre?

Purchase price, parts and services roll into one total, divided by the distance you have actually ridden. Bike Stories shows both numbers on the bike's dashboard, so an expensive bike you ride every day can turn out cheaper than a cheap one in the shed.

How do I know when to replace a chain?

Give the part an expected life when you fit it. Bike Stories counts the distance ridden since and shows the wear as a bar — green while there is life left, amber when it is nearly gone — so a worn chain gets replaced before it takes the cassette with it.

Features

A logbook that does the arithmetic for you

A notebook in the shed can hold a service history. It can't tell you what the bike costs per kilometre, or that the chain is at 43% and due before the sportive.

The true cost of ownership

Purchase, parts and every service roll up into one number per bike — and into cost per kilometre, the figure that settles arguments about whether the nice bike was worth it.

Services scheduled by date or distance

Every 500 km, or every six months, or whichever comes first. The odometer builds itself from your rides, so a distance-based service falls due as you ride — and a reminder tells you before the noise starts.

Parts, and how much life is left

Chain, tyres, brake pads, battery, bars — 13 categories, each with what it cost and what it is fitted to. Give a part an expected life and its wear shows as a bar you can read in a second.

Rides that log themselves

Cycling workouts come in from Apple Health — your watch, or whatever app writes there. Or drop in a GPX file and see the route on a map. Or type a ride in ten seconds. Whichever you choose, the odometer keeps up.

Manuals, receipts and warranties

Keep the paperwork with the bike instead of in a drawer. Insurance and registration get a renewal date, and a reminder before it lapses.

FAQ

Fair questions

Is Bike Stories free?

The app is free to download and free to use for up to two bikes, with every logging feature included: maintenance, parts, rides, costs, documents and photos. Bike Stories Pro is a one-time $9.99 in-app unlock that removes the bike limit and adds Apple Health ride import, PDF export and automatic backups. There is no subscription.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no email and no Bike Stories server holding your data. Your bikes live on your iPhone and sync between your own devices through your iCloud account, which only you can read.

Can it import my rides automatically?

Yes. Bike Stories reads cycling workouts from Apple Health, so anything that writes there — your watch, Strava, Wahoo, Garmin Connect and the rest, when you let them — can become a ride on the right bike. Turn on automatic sync and new workouts are picked up when you open the app. You can also load a GPX file and see the route on a map, or log a ride by hand in a few seconds. Health import is part of Pro; manual rides and GPX are free.

Does it work for e-bikes and mountain bikes?

Yes — there are 13 bike types, from road and gravel to mountain, city, cargo, folding and electric, and each starter template comes with a maintenance schedule that suits it. E-bikes also get battery health checks: measured capacity, estimated range and charge cycles, tracked over time.

How does it know when a service is due?

You schedule a service by date, by distance, or both — every 500 km, or every six months, whichever comes first. The bike's odometer builds itself from the rides you log, so a distance-based service becomes due as you ride. Turn on reminders and you get a notification when something falls due, rather than finding out on a climb.

What happens to my data?

It stays yours. Bikes, rides, receipts and photos are stored on your device and mirrored to your own iCloud account — nothing is uploaded to us and nothing is sold. The app does send anonymous, non-identifying usage analytics so we can see which screens people get stuck on; no bike data is ever included, and you can switch it off in Settings. The details are in the privacy policy.

Can I get my data out again?

Yes. Pro exports a bike's service and cost history as a PDF, plus a seller document for when you sell the bike — a full, dated history is worth real money at resale. Pro also keeps automatic local backups you can restore from.

What do I need to run it?

An iPhone running iOS 17 or later. Nothing else: no account, no subscription and no internet connection to read your own logbook. Distances show in kilometres or miles and costs in your own currency, and the app is available in 49 languages.
